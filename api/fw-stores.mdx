---
title: "fw_stores"
description: "Open a named store from another script, and change the shop list."
---

Runs on the client. Another script opens a named store with the event under Opening the counter. What the player presses, and what a purchase costs, is on the [shops](/player/shops) page.

Goods, Work, and Services switch only the clerk's panel. Work lists the errands this clerk will give. A store with none still says the clerk has nothing to do. How to add an errand is in [fw_missions](/api/fw-missions). Services says they are not mending or treating anyone on this visit.

Closing the counter puts the shelf back the way the store lists it. What was settled stays in the bag.

## Opening the counter

Pressing Shop at the clerk opens that store, after a short loading prompt while the visit is built. The game asks the server for that shop's shelf, then draws it. That pause is `openPauseMs` on `FwStoresConfig`, 400 milliseconds unless you change it. The Shop prompt hides while that counter is on screen, and comes back when it closes. One player holds a counter at a time. The same player may ask again. A different player gets no shelf, the loading prompt stops, and the log says someone is already at the counter. Opening a second shop lets the first one go. Closing the counter lets it go too, and so does the player leaving. A shop that is turned off has no clerk, and the open is refused before anyone takes the counter. Start `fw_stores` after `oxmysql`, `fw_satchel`, and `fw_inventory`. The tables are created on start. The screen uses the bag's pictures.

Another script on the client can open a named store. `storeId` is the store name, such as `ValentineGeneral` or `BlackwaterGeneral`. A missing name does nothing. The counter closes if that event runs again while it is already open.

```lua
--- Opens one store's counter beside the bag.
--- Nothing happens when that store is missing, or when the name is empty.
---@param storeId string
---@return nil
AddEventHandler('fw_stores:open', function(storeId)
    -- The bag screen keeps the mouse.
end)
```

```lua
TriggerEvent('fw_stores:open', 'ValentineGeneral')
```

## The store list

A shop the player can walk up to is a row in the database. Its name, town, place, clerk, hours, and the items on that counter live there. Change one from `/admin`, on Stores. The file does not list shops. A fresh database starts with none. Shops already saved keep their place. Their shelves were cleared once, and a later restart does not clear a shelf you stock after that.

A type on `FwStoresConfig.types` is the kind of shop, such as `general`. It stays in the file. So do the pay rates, who may trade, the clerk's stance and lines, and the map mark. A new kind of shop is another entry there: a label, a template name, a scenario, lines, a mark, and who may trade. A shop that names that type receives those fields. The shelf does not. That is a saved row.

`rows` on a type is how many rows that counter uses. Leave it off and the counter stays at `counter.rows`, which is 14. The gunsmith sets 20 so a rifle still packs. Raising the shared 14 would add empty rows to the general store.

Leave `pay` and `buy` off a type and that shop uses the general rates. Write them on the type when that trade should differ. `access.sell` set to a word that is not a standing, such as `Closed`, means the clerk never buys. The gunsmith is set that way. The butcher, the trapper, and the bait shop set `sell` to `Unknown`, so a first visit can still sell. The doctor and the saloon keep selling at Recognized, the same as the general store. Barter stays closed on these shelves until a type has a real trade.

`access` says who may buy, sell, and barter. `pay` is the share of the shelf price. `buy` is the share of the buyback. Those stay on the type. The items and the dollars on one counter are the saved shelf.

The general type sets `template` to `general`. That name is a shared shelf you save in the database. The server does not copy a shelf in from a file. Templates on the admin screen add, edit, and delete a shelf. A new database has none until you create one. Saving a shelf does not change shops that already copied it. New shop copies that type's saved shelf onto the new shop, when that shelf exists. Reset shelf does the same for one shop. Deleting a name a type still points at makes those two calls fail until the shelf exists again.

`location` is where the clerk stands, a `vector4` with the heading last. On a live shop that place is the saved row. `hours` belongs to that shop. It is `{ open, close }` or `false`. The first hour is included and the second is not, so `{ 6, 21 }` is 6:00 through 20:59. When the first hour is later, such as `{ 21, 6 }`, the shop stays open across midnight. `false`, or both hours left empty, means always open. Outside the hours you write, the clerk is gone, the Shop prompt is hidden, and a sale is refused with `The store is closed.` Turning the shop off does the same. If the time resource is not running, the shop stays open.

`model` is the ped. `scenario` is the stance, such as `WORLD_HUMAN_SHOPKEEPER`. `voice` is that ped's voice bank. `lines` names the greet, the browse line when the counter opens, and the sale line when the trade goes through. `takes` is how many recordings a line has. A sale picks one of those and cuts off a line already playing.

The clerk appears within `spawnDistance` (50) while the shop is open. The prompt is within `promptDistance` (2.5). Each store's `blip.range` decides when its mark is on the map. `blipRange` (120) is that distance when a store does not set its own.

`blip` is that store's mark. Leave it off and the store uses its type's mark. The `name` on the store is the words on that mark. `style` is the blip style, such as `BLIP_STYLE_DEBUG_YELLOW`. `sprite` is the icon, such as `blip_ambient_npc`. `modifiers` is a list of blip modifiers, such as `BLIP_MODIFIER_MP_COLOR_6`. `color` is a color word, such as `yellow`, when you are not listing modifiers. `range` is how close the player must be, in metres, before the mark exists. The general type's mark uses `150`. The mark stays until the player is 20 metres past that, then it leaves the map. Come back inside the range and it returns. Leave `range` off a mark you write yourself and the store uses `blipRange` (120).

`card` set to `false`, or left out, means the mark has no card. A table is the card that opens when the player hovers that mark. Selecting it keeps the card open. `type` has to be `map_card_mission`. `title` is the large line. `subtitle` is the smaller line. `description` is the body, and `~n~` starts a new line. `footer` is the region line after the player selects the mark. Hovering does not change that line. `txd` is the picture's texture dictionary, such as `map_card_val_generalstore`. `txn` is the texture name inside it. The store sample uses `image`.

A town mark is separate from the shop mark. It uses the town style and stays on the map, because it is created with no range. `FwStoresConfig.towns` names each town. `name` is the words on the mark and the card title. `coords` is where the mark sits. `footer` is the region line. `txds` is the list of card pictures. One picture is chosen when the player joins, and that picture stays until they leave and come back. Leave `txds` off when you have no picture. The texture name inside each dictionary is `image`.

On a store, `town` is the key in that table, such as `valentine`. The card lists each shop type once, in this order: General Store, Gunsmith, Doctor, Barber, Butcher, Trapper, Bait and Tackle, Saloon. A type is left off when that town has no store of that type. Two saloons in one town are one Saloon line. A store with no `town`, such as a trapper camp, does not add a line and does not get a town mark. A town with no stores is skipped. A store that names a town missing from `towns` is skipped, and the client log says so.

Saint Denis uses `nbx`, Blackwater uses `blk`, Armadillo uses `amd`, Tumbleweed uses `tbl`, and Van Horn uses `vht`. Wallace Station has one picture in the game. Lagras has two. The other towns have three.

Each line in `goods` is one pile on the counter.

- `item` is the item name, such as `consumable_can_beans`. The label, the size, and the category come from that item.
- `price` is the dollars for one, before a standing changes it. The full list is at the end of this page.
- Leave `count` off and the pile stays in stock. One purchase can take up to a full stack. A tin of beans is 20. A hatchet is 1. A shovel is 1.
- `count` is a limited pile. That number goes down on this screen when it is bought.
- `standing` is who may buy it. Leave it off and anyone can. `Recognized` is tier II, `Trusted` is tier III, and `Established` is tier IV. A player below that standing sees the pile locked.
- `price` and `barter` can sit on the same pile. `price` is the dollars. `barter` is the other way to pay, once this visit may barter. Each piece is an item and how many, such as `{ item = 'egg', count = 4 }`. The saved shelf keeps that trade. On Stores, Add trade writes it under that pile. A standing rate changes the dollars. It leaves the trade alone. A short stack does not pay for part of a trade.
- `prices` sets the dollars for one standing and skips the type rate. `prices = { Established = 1 }` means Established pays 1 dollar.

`buyback` is what the clerk pays for one item placed on Offer. A number is the list price. A table uses `price` as the list price, and a standing name when that standing should get a different amount.

```lua
egg = { price = 1, Established = 2 }
```

The general shelf lists a number for each good the clerk buys. An egg, an apple, a potato, or an ear of corn is 1 dollar. Valentine pays 2 dollars for an egg. Mint is 2. Honey, beeswax, wool, a rabbit pelt, or a fish is 4. A deer hide is 8.

On the type, `pay` is the share of `price` the player pays. `1` is the shelf price. `0.9` is nine tenths of it. `buy` is the share of the buyback the clerk pays. The result is a whole dollar. A shelf price of at least 1 dollar stays at least 1 dollar.

`access` on the type says who may buy, who may sell, and who may barter. Each line is a standing name. Leave a line off, or set `Unknown`, and everyone may do that. A word that is not a standing refuses that action. The general store lets everyone buy. Selling waits for Recognized. Barter waits for Trusted.

```lua
access = {
    buy = 'Unknown',
    sell = 'Recognized',
    barter = 'Trusted',
}
```

A pile's own `standing` still locks that pile. A tin of peaches stays Recognized even though buying, as an action, is open to everyone.

`FwStoresConfig.useReputation` is `true`. The counter asks fw_reputation for this visit's standing. Set it to `false` and every visit is Unknown again. Everyone can buy an unlocked pile. Selling and barter stay closed while the standing is below the access line. The rates and the access lines stay in the file either way.

`purchasePoints` is how many points one purchase adds to that store's class. It is one number for every shop. The count is once for the visit, when the player leaves with at least one good, not once for each item. Dollars handed back do not count. A sale that only hands goods in does not count. `0` turns it off, and that is the number written now. A tier moves when a finished mission calls `Set`.

`fw_reputation` keeps the score. `source` is the client id of the player who triggered the event. It changes every time they join. `storeType` is the type key, such as `'general'`. The class name in `fw_reputation` has to be that same word.

```lua
---@param source integer
---@param storeType string
---@return string standing
exports.fw_reputation:GetStanding(source, storeType)
```

Return `Unknown`, `Recognized`, `Trusted`, or `Established`. If `fw_reputation` is missing, the call fails, or the word is anything else, the visit stays Unknown.

### GetStore

Runs on the server. The store's id, its type, and the clerk's ground point. Nil when that store is missing. A shop that is turned off still returns the spot, so an errand can measure the distance. The clerk is not standing there while the shop is off or outside its hours. `spot` is the place you measure a distance from.

```lua
--- The store's type and the clerk's ground point.
--- Nil when that store is missing.
---@param storeId string
---@return table|nil store id, type, and spot
exports.fw_stores:GetStore(storeId)
```

```lua
local store = exports.fw_stores:GetStore('ValentineGeneral')
if store == nil then
    return
end
```

`store.type` is the class, such as `general`. `store.spot` is a coordinate.

### HoldsCounter

Runs on the server. True when this `source` is the player the counter was sent to. False when nobody holds that shop, or someone else does. A purchase has to pass this check. Call it before you treat that counter as free.

```lua
--- True when this player is the one the counter was sent to.
--- False when nobody holds it, or someone else does.
---@param source integer
---@param storeId string
---@return boolean
exports.fw_stores:HoldsCounter(source, storeId)
```

```lua
local holding = exports.fw_stores:HoldsCounter(source, 'ValentineGeneral')
if not holding then
    return
end
```

### BuildVisit

Runs on the client. The same visit the counter opens, priced for the standing they have now. Nil when that store's shelf has not arrived yet, or the shop is missing. The bag screen uses this when a score changes while the counter is open, so a newly recognized clerk can buy an egg without a second visit. Open the counter first. The shelf is the one the server sent for that shop.

```lua
--- The visit the bag screen opens, priced for this player's standing.
--- Nil when that store is missing.
---@param storeId string
---@return table|nil visit
exports.fw_stores:BuildVisit(storeId)
```

```lua
local visit = exports.fw_stores:BuildVisit('ValentineGeneral')
if visit == nil then
    return
end
```

Settle takes Offer out of the bag and places Want in the bag. Dollars leave the stack when you put them on Offer, including dollars in a pouch you are still carrying. Settle puts back whatever the price did not use. Closing the counter puts the shelf back the way the store lists it, and puts those dollars back as well. The bag keeps what the clerk already handed over.

`fw_stores:settle` is networked. The client sends one table, the deal on Offer and Want. The server settles it only when that `source` holds that store. Anyone else is refused, nothing is taken, and the log says someone is already at the counter. A shop that is turned off or outside its hours is refused first, with the closed line.

Another script on the client can open the same screen. `visit` is the clerk's goods for that store. `live` is set for you, so the bag on the right is the one the player is carrying.

```lua
TriggerEvent('fw_satchel:shopToggle', visit)
```

Running it again closes the bag. This event opens the screen. Settle is what changes the bag.

## Changing a shop

`/admin`, then Stores, is the screen for this. It needs `fw.admin`. Saving also needs `fw.admin.stores`. `fw.admin` already passes that check, so a current admin does not need a new line in `permissions.cfg`.

Search the list, then choose a shop. The editor opens in a window over the list. Close and Escape leave it without saving. The window is the name, the type, the town, the clerk model and voice, the hours, and whether the shop is open. The place is x, y, z, and the heading. Here fills those from where you are standing. The shelf is each item, its price, and the standing that may buy it. Add trade under a pile lists the items that pay for it instead of dollars, and how many of each. Leave the standing on Anyone and every visit may buy that pile. Buyback is the item and the dollars the clerk pays for one. Save writes the row. Open store closes the admin screen and opens that counter. If someone already holds it, the counter does not open. Apply template copies the shelf you pick onto this shop. Reset shelf copies the shelf named by this shop's type. Other shops keep what they already have. Delete asks first.

Templates, beside New shop, is the shared shelves. The list is the shelf id and how many items are on it. New template asks for an id, such as `general`, and starts empty. Save writes that shelf. Delete asks first. A type that still names a deleted shelf has nothing to copy until you add that id again.

A new shop needs an id, a type, a name, a clerk model, and a place. The id is a word such as `ValentineGeneral`. Creating it copies that type's saved shelf. The server refuses an item name the inventory does not know when you type a line and save.

The calls below run on the server. `source` is the client id of the player who triggered the event. It changes every time they join. Each one returns nil, and a short reason, when that player does not have `fw.admin.stores`, or the stores are not ready yet. Show the reason. Do not save the page's numbers yourself. These calls are what the screen uses.

`IsReady` says the shop list has been read. It stays no until the tables exist and the rows are loaded. The server can say fw_stores has started a moment before this is true. Keep checking for a short while.

```lua
---@return boolean ready
exports.fw_stores:IsReady()
```

```lua
local ready = exports.fw_stores:IsReady()
if not ready then
    return
end
```

`AdminList` is the shops, the types, and the towns. `stores` is `{ id, name, type, town, enabled }`. `types` is `{ id, label }`. `towns` is `{ id, name }`.

```lua
---@param source integer
---@return table|nil payload
---@return string|nil err
exports.fw_stores:AdminList(source)
```

```lua
local payload, err = exports.fw_stores:AdminList(source)
if payload == nil then
    return
end
```

`AdminRead` is that same list, plus `store` for one id. `store` is the editor: `id`, `name`, `type`, `town`, `model`, `voice`, `x`, `y`, `z`, `h`, `openHour`, `closeHour`, `enabled`, `goods`, and `buyback`. `openHour` and `closeHour` are nil when the shop stays open all day. `goods` is `{ item, price, standing, barter }`. `barter` is `{ item, count }`, or nil when that pile has no trade. `buyback` is `{ item, price }`. Nothing, and `That shop is not in the list.`, when the id is missing.

```lua
---@param source integer
---@param storeId string
---@return table|nil payload
---@return string|nil err
exports.fw_stores:AdminRead(source, storeId)
```

```lua
local payload, err = exports.fw_stores:AdminRead(source, 'ValentineGeneral')
if payload == nil then
    return
end
```

`AdminSave` writes one shop that is already in the list. Pass the editor fields. `goods` and `buyback` replace the whole shelf. A price is a whole number from 0 to 1000000. Hours are two whole hours from 0 to 23, or leave both empty. The town is empty, or a key in `FwStoresConfig.towns`. After it is saved, each connected player gets the new place. The return is the list plus that shop.

```lua
---@param source integer
---@param payload table
---@return table|nil result
---@return string|nil err
exports.fw_stores:AdminSave(source, payload)
```

```lua
local result, err = exports.fw_stores:AdminSave(source, {
    id = 'ValentineGeneral',
    name = 'Worths General Store',
    type = 'general',
    town = 'valentine',
    model = 'u_m_m_valgenstoreowner_01',
    voice = '0478_U_M_M_VALGENSTOREOWNER_01',
    enabled = true,
    x = -324.16,
    y = 803.62,
    z = 117.88,
    h = 272.93,
    openHour = '',
    closeHour = '',
    goods = {
        { item = 'consumable_can_beans', price = 5 },
    },
    buyback = {
        { item = 'egg', price = 2 },
    },
})
if result == nil then
    return
end
```

`AdminCreate` adds a shop. `id` is a new word. The type has to exist. The place, the name, and the model are required. The shelf is copied from that type's saved shelf. The return is the list plus the new shop. `That shop id is already used.` means pick another id. `That type has no shelf.` means the type names a shelf that is not in the database.

```lua
---@param source integer
---@param payload table id, type, name, model, x, y, z, h, and an optional town and voice
---@return table|nil result
---@return string|nil err
exports.fw_stores:AdminCreate(source, payload)
```

```lua
local result, err = exports.fw_stores:AdminCreate(source, {
```

The clerk and the counter use their own messages. They are networked, and they are not queued. Another script should open a counter with `fw_stores:open` and read a place with `GetStore`.

`fw_stores:requestPlaces` is the client asking for the clerks. There are no arguments. The reply is `fw_stores:places`, sent to that player: a list of `{ id, type, town, name, x, y, z, h, model, voice, hours, scenario, lines, takes, blip }`. A shop that is turned off is left off the list.

`fw_stores:requestShelf` asks for one counter. The arguments are a token and the store id. The reply is `fw_stores:shelf`, sent to that player: the token, then the shelf, or false when the shop is missing, turned off, closed, or someone else already holds that counter. The shelf is `{ id, type, goods, buyback, access, pay, buy, rows }`. A real shelf is what takes the hold. The same player may ask again. Opening another shop releases the first.

`fw_stores:releaseCounter` lets that hold go. The client sends it when the counter leaves the screen, with no arguments, and that player's shops are released. Pass a store id only to release that shop when this open claimed it and the counter never opened. It does not release a shop held by someone else. `fw_stores:settle` refuses unless `HoldsCounter` is true for that store. Nothing is taken.

## List prices

These numbers are a price guide. The server does not write them. A shelf exists only after you save it. An empty counter has nothing to buy or sell.

The number is the list price. An Unknown visit pays that many dollars. Recognized pays 95 percent, Trusted 90 percent, and Established 85 percent, rounded to a whole dollar. A price of at least 1 dollar stays at least 1 dollar.

A later job should sit under these prices. A short chore pays about 1 or 2 dollars. A day of labor pays about 4 to 8 dollars, under a tin of food. A dangerous job can approach a small tool. It should stay well under a hatchet at 80 dollars.

The shelf is food the counter keeps, household goods, a short rack of garden seed, and a few tools. The clerk does not buy these goods back. The buyback list is farm and hunt goods, and those prices stay below the shelf.
